This portfolio piece analyzes data of a consumer behavior project. The
project is a rough sketch of an idea to test how consumers would decide
under time or quantity scarcity. Participants were randomly assigned to
one of the four conditions. In each condition, participants were asked
to choose one of the two hotels for their holiday. Hotel A displays a
scarcity cue (will be discussed later) and Hotel B has mostly nothing
special. The goal is to test whether certain features of Hotel A would
appeal to participants. Participants were asked three questions
(dependent measures) plus an attention check item. The three measures
were the participant’s decision, their intention to choose Hotel A, and
the attractiveness of Hotel A. The attention check item asked the
participants to recall the discount of Hotel A. Personally speaking,
this attention check is too hard. As a matter of fact, I think whether
participants could pass it might be related to the condition they were
assigned to, which introduced a confound.

In conditions 1 and 2 (C1 and C2), both hotels were 1 mile away from the
destination. In C1, Hotel A had a quantity scarcity cue, something like
only three rooms left. In C2, Hotel A had a time scarcity cue, something
like promotions ends tonight.

In conditions 3 and 4 (C3 and C4), the distance from the hotels to the
destination was manipulated. Hotel A was 1 mile away but Hotel B was 12
miles away from the destination. Similar to C1 and C2, Hotel A had a
quantity scarcity cue in C3 and Hotel A had a time scarcity cue in C4.

It’s obvious that this pilot study is problematic. The biggest issue is
lacking a baseline condition where both hotels display no scarcity cues.
This makes the interpretation of the data extremely difficult.

However, I had no involvement in experiment design and this is what I
have. So I have to make the most of it.

The results indicated that he difference in the effect of a quantity
scarcity cue and that of a time scarcity cue was minimal with and
without a distance advantage. There are two possibilities. First, the
effect of a scarcity cue is minimal. Individuals may make similar
decisions regardless of scarcity cues in hotel booking. Second, both
quantity and time scarcity cues nudge individuals to decide, but their
effects are largely the same. But I cannot tell what’s going on because
of an absence of a baseline condition. Based on what we have,
participants’ decisions were mostly shaped by distance advantage.

I also performed a sensitivity analysis by include participants who
failed attention check. It replicated the results of excluding them.
